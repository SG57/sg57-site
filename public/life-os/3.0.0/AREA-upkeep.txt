# Upkeep & safety
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (holds and reminders only) · connectors: none · also: home, money, protection, preparedness, vehicles, home-buying, later-life · fresh_days: 365 for alarm and system dates, 30 for recalls · sensitivity: low · default: on · wakes_on: always · sleeps_when: never · label: "Upkeep"`

## Purpose
Alarms work, the home's systems get care before they fail, hidden hazards (radon, mold, lead, well water) get checked, and the things you own keep their warranties and recalls, whether you rent or own.

## Intake
1. "How old are your smoke and carbon monoxide alarms? The date is printed on the back."
2. "Owning: roughly how old are the roof, furnace or heat pump, and water heater? Renting: skip this one."
3. "City water or a private well? Sewer or septic?"
4. "Has the place had a leak, a musty smell, or a radon test that you know of?"
5. "Anything you bought lately that's still under warranty or returnable?"

## Standing watch
- **Alarms:** end-of-life dates printed on each unit; tested at each clock change.
- **Upkeep calendar:** filters, dryer vent yearly, water heater, gutters, outdoor faucets before the first freeze, HVAC service; each major system's age against its typical lifespan, with a repair reserve sized from them (money).
- **Hidden hazards:** radon retested on the interval the national agency gives (US: EPA; look up); any leak dried within a day or two and checked for mold; a private well tested yearly; septic pumped on the inspector's interval; lead questions before sanding or renovating a home built before 1978 (US).
- **Things you own:** return windows, warranties from receipts, product recalls (US: cpsc.gov), and a yearly photo or video inventory kept where the person picks.
- **Help that's gone:** when the person who did the yard, gutters, snow, or repairs is no longer there, what they did and two ways to cover each.
- **Contractors:** a written estimate and the license checked on the state board's own site before any deposit; renters' repair requests go through home.
- Firearms, only if the person raises it, never asked, and kept under the generic label 'home safety': storage under the state's child-access law (look up), transport rules before a move or trip, and in an estate, transfer only per state law (an estate attorney).
- **Costs and doing nothing:** A burst pipe: commonly $1k-$20k+ in damage (look up local repair costs); draining outdoor faucets costs nothing. Major systems: water heater commonly $1k-$3.5k, HVAC $8k-$15k, roof $15k-$25k+ (look up local quotes). Radon: a test kit commonly $15-$50 and mitigation commonly $800-$2.5k (look up local prices). Mold left behind a wall after a leak: remediation commonly runs to thousands (look up local prices).

## Annual dates
- Clock changes (US: 2nd Sun Mar, 1st Sun Nov; UK, EU: last Sun Mar and Oct; no clock change: two anchors six months apart) · one checklist: alarms, filters, recall check, vehicle recalls (vehicles), go-bag rotation (preparedness) · Any · checklist · look up if your region's rule changed
- Autumn clock change or first frost · outdoor faucets drained, water heater, gutters · Any · checklist · none
- Yearly · home inventory refresh; well water test if on a well · Any · 20-minute walk-through list · none
- Per the national agency's interval · radon retest · US (elsewhere the national agency) · reminder · look up current guidance

## Life events that touch this area
- Home purchase · upkeep calendar, radon, well, and septic dates set from the inspection report
- A leak or water damage · dried within a day or two; photos first and a claim question (preparedness, protection)
- Helper dies or moves away · the help-that's-gone list within 30 days
- A fall or turning 70 · home-fit walkthrough offered (later-life)

## What healthy looks like
"The alarms work, the hidden stuff got checked, and nothing in the house breaks without a plan for it."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "That alarm is past its date or missing; replacing it this week is the one Upkeep thing I'd do first." — an alarm past its printed end date or missing
- "Water that sits more than a day or two can grow mold out of sight, so drying it out comes first. I'll draft the repair request." — a leak or water damage the person mentions
- "Something you own has an unfixed safety recall; the fix or refund is usually free. I'll draft the request." — a recall lookup matches an item on the person's list

## Professional lane · escalate_if
A gas smell or sounding CO alarm gets the SAFETY S3.1 line, never an area line. Gas, electrical, structural, roof, and radon or mold remediation: a licensed contractor or certified professional (license checked on the state board's own site). Lead hazards: a certified lead inspector. Firearms law: an attorney. Say 'this one is for a licensed <trade>' before any repair beyond a filter or a battery.
