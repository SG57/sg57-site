# Status papers
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (reminders and calendar holds only) · connectors: none · also: work, travel, moving, paperwork, kids, partner · fresh_days: 30, and 7 inside a filing window · sensitivity: high · default: dormant · wakes_on: holding a visa or residence permit, moving abroad, job offer abroad · sleeps_when: citizenship granted and the follow-ups (passport, voter registration) done · label: "Status papers"`

## Purpose
Your right to live, work, and travel where you are never lapses because a date, an address report, or a form slipped.

## Intake
1. "What status documents do you hold, in your words? The document kind is enough; never the number."
2. "When does each card or visa expire?"
3. "Does your status depend on your job, your partner, or school?"
4. "Any travel abroad planned?"
5. "Do you have an immigration attorney or accredited representative?"

## Standing watch
- Stored only as 'a status document' with its expiry; the category never appears in a snapshot, view, or preview (discreet label 'paperwork'); last four characters of any number at most (S2.4).
- **Filing windows:** when renewal or the next step may be filed (often months before expiry; some open only 90 days before), looked up and confirmed with the attorney.
- **Address reports after a move (US:** within 10 days; look up the current rule); US registration and carry-proof rules for adults (look up the current rule).
- **Travel effects:** re-entry documents and time-abroad limits for residency or citizenship (looked up; the attorney confirms).
- **Job-based status:** any grace period after a job ends (work; the attorney confirms).
- **Family:** dependents' documents and dates, and a child's citizenship or consular paperwork when a parent isn't a citizen of the birth country (look up).
- **Costs and doing nothing:** A status that lapses: time out of status can bar a return for years (an attorney). A missed address report (US): a violation that can complicate later filings. A long trip abroad: can break continuous residence for citizenship (look up; an attorney). Filing fees: hundreds to over a thousand dollars per form (look up the current fees); a rejected form can cost months.

## Annual dates
- Per document · filing window opens (often 90 days to 6 months before expiry) · Any · hold · look up and confirm with the attorney

## Life events that touch this area
- Move · address report (US: within 10 days; look up)
- Job change or layoff · status grace period (work; an attorney)
- Trip abroad · re-entry documents and time-abroad limits
- Birth · the child's citizenship paperwork
- Citizenship granted · passport and voter registration (paperwork)

## What healthy looks like
"I always know my next status date, and nothing was filed late."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "A notario or consultant who isn't a licensed attorney or accredited representative can't give immigration legal help in the US. This one is for an immigration attorney or accredited representative." — help offered by someone who isn't an attorney or accredited representative
- "Threats about your status by message are a known scam pattern." — a message threatening deportation or status loss (identity's message line first when it's awake)
- "Your status document expires <date>, and you can already file for the next step; this can't wait for the Brief." — a filing window open with no filing and expiry inside 90 days

## Professional lane · escalate_if
Eligibility, travel effects, job changes, and any notice from the immigration agency: an immigration attorney or accredited representative (elsewhere, a regulated immigration adviser). Say 'this one is for your immigration attorney' on any question about what to file or whether to travel.
