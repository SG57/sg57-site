# Business & side income
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (reminders and calendar holds only) · connectors: none (optional: read-only bank data for the business account) · also: taxes, money, protection, retirement, digital, work, home, public-benefits · fresh_days: 30, 7 near a filing date · sensitivity: medium · default: opt-in · wakes_on: starting freelance or side-business income, forming an LLC or company, renting out a property or room, hiring help at home or for the business, onboarding (life map: your own business) · sleeps_when: the business or rental is closed and its final returns and dissolution filings are done · label: "Business"`

## Purpose
Money you earn outside a paycheck (freelance, a company, a rental) stays legal, separate, insured, priced to cover what an employer would, and taxed on time, without you becoming an accountant.

## Intake
1. "What do you earn outside a paycheck, and roughly since when?"
2. "Sole proprietor, LLC, or company? Which state? Any rental property?"
3. "Is there a separate bank account for it, and a set-aside for taxes?"
4. "Any licenses, permits, sales tax, contracts, or insurance for it?"
5. "How many clients or tenants, and does any one bring in more than a third of the money?"

## Standing watch
- **Tax set-aside:** a share of every payment moved by the person into a separate account (commonly 25-30% for US self-employment; the preparer sets the real figure); estimated-payment dates held (taxes).
- **Separate money:** a business account and receipts kept, so deductions can be shown and an LLC's protection holds.
- **Contracts and invoices:** a written agreement before work starts (scope, kill fee, late terms, who owns the work); invoices aged with follow-ups at 30, 60, and 90 days; drafted here, sent by the person.
- **Client concentration:** any client over about a third of income raised quarterly, with the cushion target sized for losing them (money).
- **Rate check:** yearly, the rate against what an employer would cover (health cover, retirement, sick days, slow months, both halves of payroll tax); portfolio refreshed with it.
- **No employer safety net:** no workers' comp, usually no unemployment, no paid sick time, so disability, liability or errors-and-omissions, equipment cover, and the home-business gap in a renters or home policy go to protection as questions; a home-office setup that won't injure you.
- **Filings:** business license, DBA, permits, LLC annual report, sales tax, and 1099-NEC to contractors (VERIFIED from the issuing office or state registry); ownership-reporting rules looked up each year.
- **Hiring:** employer registration, payroll tax, and household-employee rules for a nanny or caregiver at home, looked up before the first payday (a CPA).
- **Rental profile:** lease dates, deposit-return deadlines, landlord insurance, the local rental license or short-term permit, and income and expense records for taxes.
- **Costs and doing nothing:** Missed estimated payments (US): an underpayment penalty that works like interest (look up the current rate). Self-employment tax (US): both halves of Social Security and Medicare, about 15.3% on top of income tax (look up the current rate). A missed LLC annual report: late fees commonly $50-$500, then dissolution (look up the state). Unpaid invoices: the longer they sit, the less likely they are ever collected. A home business with no business cover: home policies often exclude business losses (look up your policy's wording).

## Annual dates
- Mid-Jan, mid-Apr, mid-Jun, mid-Sep · estimated payments (taxes owns) · US · set-aside check · look up current dates
- Late Jan · 1099-NEC to contractors over the threshold · US · list for the preparer · look up the current threshold
- Per state · LLC annual report, license and permit renewals, sales tax · US-state · hold 30 days ahead · look up the state's rules
- Yearly · rate check and client mix · Any · one-page draft · none

## Life events that touch this area
- First self-employed income · separate account and the estimates question within 30 days
- Forming an LLC · state filing, registered agent, annual report date, business account
- Renting out a property or room · landlord cover before the tenant moves in; local permit before listing (look up)
- Big client leaves · cushion check and pipeline list the same week (money)

## What healthy looks like
"Tax money is already set aside, every client has a contract, and no filing date sneaks up."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "'Your business filing is late' notices that ask for a fee are a known scam pattern." — a compliance or registration fee demand by message (identity's message line first when it's awake)
- "That's a tax decision; I'll list the questions, and a CPA answers." — 'should I become an S-corp?' or 'can I write this off?'
- "Starting work before there's a signed agreement leaves nothing to point to if the client doesn't come through. I'll draft one from your terms for an attorney to check." — new client work with no written contract

## Professional lane · escalate_if
Entity choice, deductions, payroll, and sales tax: a CPA or enrolled agent. Contracts, liability, partnership terms, tenant screening, and evictions: a business or landlord-tenant attorney. Cover: an insurance broker. Say 'this one is for <professional>' when the question turns from when to which.
