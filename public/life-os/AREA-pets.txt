# Pets
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (reminders and calendar holds only) · connectors: none (optional: read-only calendar) · also: protection, estate, money, travel, moving, home, home-buying · fresh_days: 30 for medicines, 365 for vaccines · sensitivity: medium · default: opt-in · wakes_on: new pet, onboarding (life map: animals at home) · sleeps_when: no animals in the household, after refills, auto-ship, and insurance are closed · label: "Pets"`

## Purpose
The animals are cared for on time, their costs are planned, and someone is ready to step in tomorrow if you can't.

## Intake
1. "What animals live with you? Species and age is enough; a name only if you want me to use it."
2. "Who is your vet, and is there a 24-hour animal hospital near you?"
3. "Any daily medicine or prevention? The label wording is enough."
4. "Is there pet insurance, or money set aside for vet emergencies? When does the policy renew?"
5. "If you were away or in hospital, who would look after them?"

## Standing watch
- **Names:** pet names are common security answers, so they live only in the ledger and on the care-plan card, never in the snapshot, view, or any shared file; elsewhere use the species.
- **Vaccines, prevention, and wellness exams:** due dates VERIFIED from the vet record; prevention on an anchor with a window (R11); the person books.
- Medicine refills from the label; nudge at 7 days left.
- **Emergency money:** pet insurance against a self-funded vet fund, decided at adoption and reviewed at renewal (protection owns the policy).
- **Microchip registry:** phone and address checked yearly and within 30 days of a move, by the person in the registry itself.
- Care-plan card (a section of estate's emergency packet) and backup caregiver: a sitter or boarding booked as soon as travel or wedding dates exist (peak seasons book out months ahead), a backup for a sudden hospital stay, and daily-care help when the person who walked, lifted, or medicated the animal is gone.
- Pet guardian named in the will with how care is funded (estate owns the document), re-asked yearly against the owner's own age and health and the animal's age.
- **Housing rules:** pet clauses at lease signing and renewal, move-in photos (home), and insurer or HOA breed and count limits checked before buying a home (home-buying).
- **Changes at home:** a gradual introduction plan from the vet or a trainer before a new person or animal joins the home; on move day, the animals kept away from open doors.
- **Costs and doing nothing:** Emergency vet visit: commonly $500-$5k+ (look up local); pet insurance commonly $15-$90 a month by species and age (look up quotes). Boarding or sitters in peak season: commonly $30-$90 a night per animal, booked out months ahead (look up local). Out-of-date microchip details: a found animal can't be matched to you. No care plan or guardian: animals can go to a shelter if you're suddenly unable to care for them. Aftercare when an animal dies: cremation or burial commonly $50-$500+ (look up local prices).

## Annual dates
- Per vet · vaccines and heartworm · Any · hold the vet's due dates · vet record
- Yearly and after a move · microchip registry details · Any · reminder · the registry
- Per policy · pet insurance renewal (protection owns) · Any · keep-or-switch questions · read the policy
- Per local law · rabies and pet license · US · hold the date · look up the current rule

## Life events that touch this area
- New pet · first vet visit by the adoption papers' date; insurance or vet fund decided (protection)
- Trip or wedding dates set · sitter or boarding booked; care-plan card at least 14 days before
- Hospital stay · care-plan card the same day
- Move · registry within 30 days; animals contained on move day
- A new person or animal joining the home · introduction plan by a date you pick, stored as 'pet errand'
- Death of a pet · that animal's items marked handled at once; nothing new for 14 days except cancellations on your yes

## What healthy looks like
"No missed doses, vet reminders get booked, and someone could step in tomorrow, card in hand."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "This one is for your vet or an emergency animal hospital, right now." — not eating 24 hours, swollen belly with retching, something toxic eaten, trouble breathing, collapse, a seizure, straining to pee with nothing coming out, heavy bleeding, hit by a car, overheating
- "That's a question for your vet; please don't change anything until they say so." — changing, skipping, doubling, or adding a dose, or human medicine for a pet
- "A bite that broke the skin is one for your doctor, right now." — the animal bit someone and broke the skin; heavy bleeding gets the SAFETY S3.1 line first
- "Lost animals are most often found through their microchip registry and local shelters. I'll draft the registry update and a lost-pet notice for you." — a lost pet

## Professional lane · escalate_if
The vet decides diagnosis, treatment, doses, diet, and prevention: 'this one is for your vet'. Behavior around a new baby: the vet or a certified trainer. Claims: the insurer; disputes: the insurance regulator or an attorney. Dissent duty (S3.5) covers money only, never treatment or end-of-life choices.
