# Digital life
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (reminders and calendar holds only) · connectors: none · also: identity, estate, business, after-loss, separation, partner · fresh_days: 30 for backups · sensitivity: medium · default: opt-in · wakes_on: new phone or computer, onboarding (first month; anyone with photos or work on a device) · sleeps_when: never once awake; quiet months are normal · label: "Digital life"`

## Purpose
Your photos, files, and accounts survive a lost phone, a dead drive, a lapsed domain, or a hospital stay, and someone you chose can reach what they need if you can't.

## Intake
1. "If your phone fell in a lake today, what would you lose?"
2. "Where do your photos and important files live, and is there a second copy somewhere else?"
3. "Have you set a legacy or inactive-account contact with your phone and email providers?"
4. "If you were in the hospital for a week, could someone you trust pay a bill or read an urgent email for you? Yes or no is enough."
5. "Do you own a website address, or an email address on your own domain?"

## Standing watch
- **Backups:** the last backup date per device, VERIFIED when the person reads it from the device's own settings, fresh for 30 days; three copies, two kinds of storage, one away from home; business files and client work checked quarterly.
- **Storage and domains:** renewal dates with auto-renew on, VERIFIED from the provider's own billing page; a lapsed domain lets a stranger receive the person's mail.
- **Device support:** the date security updates end, looked up from the maker yearly.
- Access if you can't, while alive (a section of estate's emergency packet): a documented way for a chosen person to reach accounts during surgery or a coma (password-manager emergency access, shared bill-pay, a POA the bank accepts); legacy contacts only work after a death.
- **Access after death:** legacy contacts with phone, email, and photo providers, and a digital-assets list (account kinds and where instructions live, never passwords) for the executor (estate).
- **Someone else's accounts after a death:** closed or memorialized through each provider's own process with the death certificate; subscriptions stopped (after-loss).
- **Untangling after a separation:** shared passwords, cloud albums, family plans, streaming, and location sharing reviewed in the person's own settings (S3.7 and discreet mode first); never into the other person's accounts.
- **Old accounts holding saved cards or personal data:** a yearly list; closing is the person's tap.
- **Costs and doing nothing:** A lost phone with no backup: years of photos gone; a backup commonly costs $0-$10 a month (look up current plans). A lapsed domain: mail stops and a stranger can register it; renewal commonly $10-$50 a year (look up your registrar). Recovering a failed drive: commonly $300-$3k with no guarantee (look up local services). No access plan while you're incapacitated: bills bounce and accounts freeze for weeks, exactly when help is needed.

## Annual dates
- Birthday month · legacy contacts, emergency access, recovery info · Any · checklist · none
- Quarterly · backup check, business files included · Any · one-line check · none
- Per provider · storage plan and domain renewals · Any · hold 30 days ahead · the provider's billing page

## Life events that touch this area
- New phone or computer · backup confirmed within 7 days; the old device wiped only after
- Surgery or hospital stay planned · emergency access checked before
- Separation · own settings untangled within 7 days, after the safety check
- Death of someone close · their accounts handled through each provider's process (after-loss)

## What healthy looks like
"My photos exist in two places, my devices still get updates, and someone I trust could get in if I couldn't."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "The last backup I can confirm is from <date>, so anything since then exists only on this device." — a repair, trade-in, wipe, or trip with a backup older than 30 days
- "'Storage full', 'domain expiring', and 'account deleted' messages are a common scam pattern." — 'your storage is full', 'your domain expires', or 'your account will be deleted' messages (identity's message line first when it's awake)
- "I never hold passwords or codes, even for someone you trust; your password manager's emergency-access feature is built for exactly this." — asked to store or pass on a password or code

## Professional lane · escalate_if
Recovery from a damaged drive: a professional recovery service before home fixes. Who legally inherits accounts or crypto keys: an estate attorney. A business data breach: an attorney. Say 'this one is for <professional>' before any repair attempt or legal question.
