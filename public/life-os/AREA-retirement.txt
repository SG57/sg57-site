# Retirement
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (reminders and calendar holds only) · connectors: none (optional: read-only brokerage data) · also: money, taxes, protection, work, later-life, estate, partner · fresh_days: 30 for statements, 365 for the earnings record · sensitivity: high · default: opt-in · wakes_on: first job, new job, retirement or investment account opened or found, stock grant or options, turning 50, retiring within 10 years, retiring within 12 months, onboarding (life map: a retirement account) · sleeps_when: never once an account or pension exists · label: "Retirement"`

## Purpose
Every match dollar is captured, no old account is lost, your earnings record is right, and every one-way choice (claiming, pension, rollovers, withdrawals) is made with your own numbers and a professional.

## Intake
1. "Does your job offer a retirement plan with a match? Do you know the formula?"
2. "Any old retirement accounts from past jobs, IRAs, a pension, or investment accounts?"
3. "Have you read your Social Security statement (US) or national pension record lately?"
4. "Roughly when would you like to stop working or cut back?"
5. "Do you work with an advisor, or do it yourself?"

## Standing watch
- **Match capture:** rate against the formula, enrolled in the first window (VERIFIED from the plan document and payslip; an unverified formula blocks the nudge, S4.4); asked whether the match continues during unpaid leave.
- **Accounts map:** retirement, brokerage, crypto, and work equity by type, with fees and beneficiaries (protection); leaving a job means a direct rollover, never a cash-out (60-day clock if indirect).
- **Room you can't get back:** IRA or Roth while income is low; SEP or solo 401(k) for business income; catch-up at 50 and the higher catch-up at 60-63, re-elected at every new employer (look up limits).
- **Quiet cuts:** contributions lowered for care or childcare are raised again at the next review.
- **Earnings record:** the government's statement, read yearly.
- **Claiming (US Social Security, 62 to 70):** own estimates, the earnings test if working early, spousal and survivor effects modeled for both partners, a divorced-spouse benefit after a 10-year marriage; the person decides with an advisor.
- Medicare windows, Part D, and the bridge to 65 belong to protection; this department lines up claiming and income around them.
- **One-way choices for a professional:** pension single-life against joint-and-survivor, company-stock treatment at separation, Roth conversions in low-income gap years, paying off the mortgage before retiring, income-based Medicare premiums, and the RMD age with its two-in-one-year trap (look up).
- **Costs and doing nothing:** A missed match: commonly 3-6% of pay a year, compounding for decades (the plan governs). A cash-out before 59 and a half (US): income tax plus an extra 10% in many cases, often about a third of the balance (look up the exceptions). Claiming at 62: about 30% less each month than at full retirement age and roughly 40-45% less than at 70, for life (look up your own estimates).

## Annual dates
- Mid-Apr · prior-year IRA deadline (SEP with extensions) · US · reminder · look up the date and limit
- Dec 31 · workplace contributions; required withdrawals · US · reminder · look up current rules
- Yearly · Social Security statement · US · read it together · ssa.gov
- Each birthday · milestones (50, 60-63, 62, full retirement age, 70, RMD age; Medicare at 64 and 9 months is protection's) · US · list · look up current ages

## Life events that touch this area
- Leaving a job · rollover decided within 60 days; vesting checked before the last day
- Divorce · a qualified order before any split (separation; an attorney)
- Retiring within 12 months · pension election, health bridge to 65 (work holds retiree elections)
- Death of a spouse · survivor benefit against own; inherited-account options for a CPA (after-loss)

## What healthy looks like
"I get the full match, I know where every account is, and no window or one-way choice sneaks up on me."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "Retirement money isn't spending money; moving or cashing it out is an investment and tax decision. I'll write the questions for a licensed advisor or CPA." — 'should I cash out, borrow from, or move my retirement money?'
- "High-pressure sales of annuities or 'guaranteed' products, especially at a free-meal seminar, are a known pattern aimed at retirees. This one is for a fee-only fiduciary advisor before you sign." — an annuity or investment pitch with a deadline, a free-meal seminar, or a surrender charge
- "When to claim Social Security is a big one-way decision. I'll lay out your own numbers and the questions for an advisor." — 'should I claim now?'

## Professional lane · escalate_if
What to buy, sell, or roll: a fee-only fiduciary advisor. Taxes of rollovers, withdrawals, and conversions: a CPA. Plan rules: the plan administrator. Divorce splits: an attorney. Say 'this one is for <professional>' before any one-way election.
