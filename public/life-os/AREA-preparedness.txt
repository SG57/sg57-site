# Emergencies
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (reminders and calendar holds only) · connectors: none · also: home, protection, pets, health, paperwork · fresh_days: 365, and 1 during a warning · sensitivity: low · default: opt-in · wakes_on: severe weather warning or evacuation order, home damage or a break-in, onboarding (first two months; life map: hazards where you live) · sleeps_when: never once awake; quiet between seasons · label: "Emergencies"`

## Purpose
If you had to leave tonight, everyone (animals included) knows the plan, the bag is packed, the papers come with you, your insurance matches the risks where you live, and after a fire, flood, storm, or break-in the claim steps happen in the right order.

## Intake
1. "What disasters are common where you live? If you're not sure, I'll look up the official hazard maps."
2. "Does your home or renters policy cover floods or earthquakes? Most standard policies don't."
3. "Water, food, medicines, and a go-bag for a few days: yes, some, or no?"
4. "Where would everyone meet, and how would you reach each other if phones are down?"
5. "If a neighbor had to step in at your place, could they find the water shutoff, the breaker, and the animals' food?"

## Standing watch
- **Hazard profile:** looked up from official hazard maps for the address (US: FEMA flood maps and state wildfire and quake maps), VERIFIED with fetch date.
- **Cover gaps:** flood and earthquake usually need separate policies, and US flood policies often have a 30-day wait, so the question goes to protection before the season.
- **Go-bag and copies:** supplies from the official checklist (US: Ready.gov), rotated at each clock change; copies of ID, lease or deed, insurance cards, prescriptions, and pet records in a fireproof place plus an off-site or encrypted copy (location only).
- **Household runbook (a section of estate's emergency packet):** utility shutoffs, breaker, vet, insurers, and where the animals' records are, written by the person and kept where they pick; codes and passwords never stored here; reviewed yearly.
- **Medicines and power-dependent equipment:** extra supply and the utility's medical-needs program (look up).
- **Plan:** meeting place, out-of-area contact, evacuation routes, and the animals (REPORTED).
- **Home damage (fire, water, storm, break-in):** photos before cleanup, the policy's additional-living-expense cover, the claim deadline from the policy, a police report for theft; after a declared disaster, the federal assistance window (often 60 days from the declaration; look up), applied for even when insured.
- **Costs and doing nothing:** Flood with no flood policy: often not covered at all, and a few inches of water can cost $10ks (look up local). A starter kit: commonly $50-$300 (look up the official checklist). No document copies: replacing ID, proof of cover, and records takes weeks, right when they're needed.

## Annual dates
- Before the local season (hurricane, wildfire, winter) · cover check and kit rotation · Any · checklist · look up the season dates
- Clock changes · go-bag rotation, on upkeep's one checklist · Any · checklist · none
- Yearly · runbook and document copies refreshed · Any · checklist · none

## Life events that touch this area
- Move to a new area · hazard profile and cover check within 30 days
- Severe weather warning · within 72 hours: refills, documents, animals; official orders come first
- Home damage or a break-in · same day: photos before cleanup, police report for theft, the claim deadline and living-expense cover (protection)

## What healthy looks like
"The bag is packed, the papers are copied, and everyone knows where to meet."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "There's an official evacuation order for your area. Your safety comes before anything I'm tracking." — the person reports an evacuation order
- "After a disaster, contractors who show up asking for big cash deposits are a known scam pattern. I'll draft the questions for any contractor you choose." — door-to-door repair pitches after damage
- "Generators, grills, and fuel heaters indoors or in a garage can fill a home with carbon monoxide." — planning to run a generator, grill, or fuel heater indoors or in a garage

## Professional lane · escalate_if
Cover choices: an insurance agent. A disputed claim: a public adjuster or an attorney. Orders and shelters: local emergency management. A gas smell or sounding CO alarm: the SAFETY S3.1 line, never an area line.
