# Debts & credit
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (reminders and calendar holds only) · connectors: none (optional: read-only bank data) · also: money, identity, education, home-buying, vehicles, partner · fresh_days: 30 · sensitivity: high · default: opt-in · wakes_on: leaving school with student loans, first credit card, asked to co-sign, planning a mortgage or car loan, onboarding (life map: debt that charges interest) · sleeps_when: no debt and no loan planned for 12 months (the yearly report check stays with identity) · label: "Debts & credit"`

## Purpose
Every loan has a plan you chose, promo rates never flip on you, student-loan paperwork never lapses, and nobody co-signs or borrows without seeing the whole bill.

## Intake
1. "Which debts do you have? Kind and rough size is enough."
2. "Any 0% or promo rate, and when does it end?"
3. "Student loans: who's the servicer, and what plan are you on?"
4. "Do you pay the full card statement each month, or carry a balance?"
5. "Has anyone asked you to co-sign, or have you co-signed for someone?"

## Standing watch
- **Debt inventory:** each debt's rate, minimum, and due date (VERIFIED from the statement) and the payoff method the person chose (smallest first or highest rate first); never a suggestion to borrow to pay debt.
- **Cards:** paying the statement balance in full each cycle keeps the grace period; a carried balance charges interest from the purchase date; due dates held.
- **Promo expiry:** end date and whether interest is deferred (VERIFIED from the agreement), raised 60 days ahead; buy-now-pay-later due dates from the provider's own app.
- **Student loans:** the grace period end (about 6 months after leaving school for US federal loans), a plan chosen before the first bill, recertification dates, and forgiveness certifications; the official programs are free (VERIFIED from the servicer's or program's own site; rules change, so look up).
- **Co-signing:** before any signature, the full liability spelled out (all of it if they don't pay, on your credit too); existing co-signed loans tracked; a life-cover question when a co-signer would be left holding a loan (protection).
- **Big-loan readiness:** 60-90 days before a mortgage or car loan, both applicants' debts shared openly (reports and freezes: identity), and how lenders read 1099 or variable income (often two years of returns) checked a year ahead.
- **Collections:** validation rights and the time-limit question raised before any payment.
- **Costs and doing nothing:** A carried card balance: commonly 20-30% a year (look up your rate on the statement). A deferred-interest promo not paid off in time: back interest from day one can post at once (the agreement governs). A missed student-loan recertification or the wrong plan: a higher payment, interest added to the balance, or a missed first payment that dents credit (look up current rules). Debt-settlement companies often charge 15-25% of enrolled debt; nonprofit credit counseling is free to low-cost (look up NFCC member agencies).

## Annual dates
- Yearly, if on an income-based plan · student-loan recertification · US · reminder 30 days ahead · the servicer's notice
- 60 days before each promo end · promo expiry · Any · payoff plan draft · the card agreement

## Life events that touch this area
- Leaving school with student loans · repayment plan chosen before the grace period ends, about 6 months after leaving
- Planning a mortgage or car loan · debts shared between applicants 60-90 days ahead; reports and freezes (identity)
- Asked to co-sign · the liability sheet before any signature
- Job loss · money's crisis order; servicer relief options looked up
- Behind on rent or mortgage · a HUD-approved housing counselor (free) before the next missed payment (home)

## What healthy looks like
"Every debt has a plan I chose, no promo surprises me, and my student loans are on the plan I picked."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "Companies that charge before settling or repairing anything are a known trap. I'll lay out the free nonprofit options first." — a debt-settlement or credit-repair pitch
- "Before any money goes to a collector, there's a time-limit question and a right to ask them to prove the debt. This one is for a consumer attorney or legal aid." — a collector asks for money on an old debt
- "Co-signing makes you responsible for the whole loan if they don't keep up, and it shows on your credit." — asked to co-sign a loan, card, or lease

## Professional lane · escalate_if
Repayment plans and budgets: a nonprofit credit counselor (US: an NFCC member agency). Student-loan plans: the servicer, then the student-aid ombudsman. A collector threatening court or wages: a consumer attorney or legal aid. Bankruptcy: a bankruptcy attorney; the required counseling only from court-approved agencies (look up). Say 'this one is for <professional>' when a debt is disputed or a lawsuit is mentioned.
