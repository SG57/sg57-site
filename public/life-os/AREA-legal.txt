# Legal papers
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (reminders and calendar holds only) · connectors: none · also: money, home, work, credit, after-loss, separation, paperwork · fresh_days: 1 for a court date · sensitivity: high · default: dormant · wakes_on: legal paper arrives, severance agreement offered, eviction or foreclosure notice, an arrest or jailing (yours or someone close), a dispute you may take further, a contract that renews on its own · sleeps_when: no open legal deadline and no contract with a notice date in the next 12 months · label: "Legal papers"`

## Purpose
Any paper with a deadline from a court, agency, or other side (a summons, ticket, demand letter, contract renewal, or small claim) gets read, its printed date gets met, and a professional decides what to do.

## Intake
1. "What paper arrived, and what date is printed on it? (The day a court paper arrives, ask only this and question 5.)"
2. "Any contracts that renew on their own: gym, phone, alarm, software, timeshare?"
3. "Anything under warranty worth tracking?"
4. "Any dispute you're thinking of taking further: a deposit, a bad repair, an unpaid invoice?"
5. "Do you have a lawyer, or is legal aid more likely?"

## Standing watch
- **Court dates and response deadlines:** VERIFIED only from the paper or the court's own records; held the day the paper arrives.
- **Demand letters and threats:** claims until a court record shows otherwise.
- **Contracts register:** renewal and cancellation-notice windows (VERIFIED from the contract text the person pastes).
- **Limitation periods on the person's own claims:** looked up and raised as a question for an attorney, never stated as the answer.
- **Small claims:** limits and filing rules looked up when a dispute is live.
- **Someone close is jailed:** their bills, lease, and car paused, a POA if they want one, money only through the facility's official service; the assistant never looks anyone up (S2.4).
- **If you're charged:** court dates held, a public defender or criminal attorney, and an immigration attorney before any plea if status applies; record sealing looked up later.
- **Costs and doing nothing:** An ignored lawsuit: a default judgment, then possible wage garnishment or a bank levy; response deadlines are often 20-30 days (the paper governs). A missed cancellation window: another year of fees, commonly $100-$1k+ (the contract governs). A missed limitation period: the claim can be gone for good. Legal help: legal aid is free if eligible; lawyers commonly $150-$500+ an hour, and many offer a low-cost first consultation (look up local).

## Annual dates
- Per contract · cancellation-notice windows · Any · hold 30 days ahead · the contract

## Life events that touch this area
- Summons or complaint served · the printed response date (often 20-30 days)
- Ticket · the court date or deadline printed on it
- Named executor · probate timing (after-loss)
- An arrest or jailing (yours or someone close) · the court date printed on the papers; an attorney the same day

## What healthy looks like
"No court date or cancellation window passed unseen, and every legal question went to a lawyer."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "This looks like a court paper, and court papers carry a deadline. Let's find that date first; this one is for an attorney or legal aid." — a summons, complaint, or court notice
- "Threats of arrest or lawsuits by message are a known scam pattern." — a lawsuit or arrest threat by text, phone, or email (identity's message line first when it's awake)
- "Signing something that waives rights or settles a claim is for an attorney before you sign." — a release, settlement, or arbitration agreement

## Professional lane · escalate_if
Anything that names a court, sets a deadline to respond, or asks for a signature that waives rights: an attorney, legal aid, or a lawyer-referral service. Say 'this one is for an attorney' the moment a court or a waiver is involved.
