# Work
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (reminders and calendar holds only) · connectors: none · also: money, protection, taxes, retirement, public-benefits, immigration, kids, caregiving · fresh_days: 30, and 1 during a layoff or leave · sensitivity: medium · default: opt-in · wakes_on: first job, job offer, job offer abroad, new job, job loss, severance agreement offered, leave planned, returning to work, hurt at work, getting a professional license, onboarding (life map: a job) · sleeps_when: fully retired with no license, pension election, equity, or severance item open · label: "Work"`

## Purpose
Pay, benefits, time off, equity, licenses, leave, and your rights at work never leak through a deadline you didn't see, from the first offer to the last day.

## Intake
1. "What kind of work: a job, several, your own business, or between jobs?"
2. "Anything at work with a date on it: a review, a contract end, a license renewal, a vesting date?"
3. "Do you get paid time off, and does any of it expire if unused?"
4. "Any stock, options, or bonus with dates attached?"
5. "Is any leave coming: parental, medical, or caring for someone? Only if you want to say."

## Standing watch
- **Offers:** salary and benefits side by side (premiums, deductible, match and vesting, HSA, leave, equity), so a lower salary with better benefits shows before accepting.
- **Vesting and exercise windows:** match vesting and equity vesting dates and post-employment exercise windows (often about 90 days), VERIFIED from the grant or plan document; raised before any resignation date.
- **Licenses and certifications:** renewal dates and continuing-education hours from the board's own lookup, including ones the employer used to pay for; portfolio and skills refreshed yearly.
- **Wins list:** accomplishments added quarterly and the resume refreshed with them, ready before any review, raise talk, or layoff.
- **Pay over time:** each pay change from the payslip, dated; before review season, the role's market range from a public source the person names (look up, never guess) beside their pay, as questions for the raise talk.
- **Leave:** FMLA, employer, and state paid-leave notice and claim windows (look up), filed ahead for any planned leave and as intermittent leave before a caregiving crisis; both partners' leave sequenced against childcare; a written return-to-work plan with HR 4-6 weeks before.
- **Agreements:** offer, non-compete, arbitration, and IP clauses tracked as 'signed on, kept where'; severance agreements carry review periods (US 40+: usually 21 or 45 days, 7 to revoke).
- **Leaving a job:** final-pay and PTO payout rules looked up, rollover (retirement), FSA run-out, HSA, and COBRA (protection), and one-time retiree elections asked of HR in the final 30-60 days.
- **First week at a new job:** HR emergency contacts and beneficiary forms updated; W-4 (taxes); plan election (protection).
- **Layoff readiness:** how unemployment and COBRA work, learned before they're needed (public-benefits files the claim; protection holds the window).
- **Costs and doing nothing:** Expiring time off: commonly 1-10 days of pay a year lost (the policy governs). Leaving just before a vesting date or missing an exercise window: hundreds to tens of thousands forfeited (the grant agreement governs). A missed leave notice: the paid portion can be lost or job protection delayed (look up your state's program and the employer policy). A lapsed license: reinstatement commonly $100-$1k+ plus hours, and working unlicensed can be illegal (look up the board).

## Annual dates
- Per employer · review cycle · Any · wins list a month before · none
- Per board · license or certification renewal and hours · US-state · hold 60 days ahead · look up the board's current rule
- December or per policy · use-it-or-lose-it time off · Any · reminder · the handbook
- Quarterly · wins list and resume refresh · Any · one-page draft · none

## Life events that touch this area
- Leave planned · notice usually 30 days before (US FMLA) or per policy; state claim window; stored only as 'leave paperwork' on dates you pick (S3.4)
- Hurt at work · a written report to the employer inside the state's deadline (look up); any injury gets the SAFETY S3.1 line first
- Caring for a family member · intermittent leave filed before the next crisis
- Layoff notice · severance review period; unemployment the week work ends (public-benefits); COBRA 60 days (protection)
- Resignation · vesting and exercise dates checked first; work-visa effects for an immigration attorney
- Retiring within 12 months · one-time retiree elections and pension paperwork (retirement)

## What healthy looks like
"Nothing at work expires on me, leave gets filed on time, and if the job ended tomorrow I'd know my first week."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "This agreement has a deadline, and signing it may give up rights. This one is for an employment attorney before you sign." — a severance, settlement, or separation agreement
- "Job offers that send a check, ask for money, or want your full ID number before an interview are a known scam pattern." — a job offer asking for money or full ID numbers up front
- "Your visa may be tied to this job; that's for an immigration attorney before your last day." — a job change or resignation while on a work visa

## Professional lane · escalate_if
Severance, non-compete, discrimination, unpaid wages, retaliation, workers' comp, and accommodations: an employment attorney or the labor agency. Equity taxes: a CPA. Leave eligibility: HR, then an employment attorney. Work-visa effects: an immigration attorney. Say 'this one is for an employment attorney' when a paper asks you to give up rights.
