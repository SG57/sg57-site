# Vehicles
`version: 3.0.0 · autonomy_max: T2 (reminders and calendar holds only) · connectors: none · also: protection, money, paperwork, moving, later-life, kids · fresh_days: 365 for registration, 30 for recalls · sensitivity: low · default: opt-in · wakes_on: buying or leasing a car, child turns 16, onboarding (life map: a car) · sleeps_when: no vehicle and no driver in the household for 90 days, after title, plate, and insurance are closed · label: "Car"`

## Purpose
Every vehicle stays legal, insured, and maintained, recalls get fixed for free, and every driver from the first permit to the last renewal is covered.

## Intake
1. "What do you drive, and roughly what year?"
2. "When are registration and inspection due? The date is on the card or sticker."
3. "Who insures it, and when does that renew?"
4. "Paid off, financed, or leased? If leased, when does it end and how many miles are allowed?"
5. "Any warning light, noise, or repair you've been putting off?"

## Standing watch
- **Registration and inspection:** dates VERIFIED from the current card or notice, held 30 days ahead, and updated inside the state window after a move or a name change.
- **Insurance continuity:** renewal (protection owns), proof in the car, and no lapse even for a month, since lapses raise future quotes; address and name changes reported; student-away discounts asked for.
- **Maintenance:** oil, tires, and brakes on the owner's-manual interval (mileage REPORTED).
- **Recalls:** a VIN check at each clock change (US: nhtsa.gov/recalls); the repair is free at a dealer.
- **Car seats:** a check at a certified fitting station on a date the person picks, and whether the car fits the seat (and a second one later).
- **Lease and loan:** mileage allowance, lease-end date, and the title or lien release after payoff.
- **Getting older:** the state's renewal and vision-test rules looked up, safety features asked about at the next purchase; whether to keep driving is the person's and their doctor's call, never reported to anyone.
- **Tickets and tolls:** real ones only from the court's or agency's own site; a text about an unpaid toll is a claim.
- **After a crash:** any injury gets the SAFETY S3.1 line first; photos, the other driver's insurer, a police report where the state requires one (look up), the insurer told promptly per the policy; rental, total-loss, and gap-cover questions go to protection.
- **Selling privately:** title signed over and the state's release-of-liability notice filed (look up); after a license action, any SR-22 filing period held (look up the state).
- **Costs and doing nothing:** Expired registration: tickets commonly $25-$200+ and a possible tow (look up your state). A coverage lapse: fines, possible suspension, and higher quotes for years (look up your state's rules). Skipped oil changes: $50-$120 each against $3k-$10k for an engine (look up local prices). Over-mileage on a lease: commonly $0.10-$0.30 a mile (the lease governs).

## Life events that touch this area
- Buying or leasing a car · insurance in force before driving away; registration in the state window
- Teen gets a permit · insurance question before the first drive
- Move to another state · license and registration in the state's window (often 10-30 days; look up)
- Car seat needed · fitted at a fitting station by a date you pick, stored as 'car errand'
- A car crash · same day: photos, police report per state rule, insurer told per the policy (protection)
- Giving up driving · getting-around plan within 30 days (later-life)

## What healthy looks like
"The car is legal, insured, and serviced, and no recall or renewal surprises me."

## Red flags
First-sentence rules (SAFETY S3.0): each quoted line opens the reply, word for word, all its sentences, by itself.
- "Unpaid-toll and ticket texts are a common scam pattern." — a toll, ticket, or registration demand by text or email (identity's message line first when it's awake)
- "There's an unfixed safety recall on this car. Recall repairs cost you nothing at a dealer; I'll draft the request." — the recall lookup shows an open recall

## Professional lane · escalate_if
Mechanical diagnosis: a licensed mechanic. A crash with injury or a disputed claim: the insurer, then an attorney. A ticket that could suspend a license: a traffic attorney. Lemon-law questions: a consumer attorney. Driving fitness: the person's doctor.
